HOW TO SCORE, 1 TO 10
Ask three questions about every line and score from the answers, not from how well it is written.
1. Is a shared reference or a real specific doing the work? A film, a chain restaurant, a fanbase, a place that deserves it, a famous syntax, the bet's own number. Yes → start at 8. An original image, however elegant → start at 6.
2. Is the attitude mean, weary, or dumb-sincere? Mean at a fanbase or a city, weary at the news, sincerely impressed by something stupid → +1. Gracious, generous, admiring the subject cleverly → −1.
3. Would he cut words? If a shorter version of the line is obviously the line, score the shorter version and take one off for the padding. A 17-word first-person confession is fine if every word is the confession; a 12-word line with a 4-word joke inside it is a 6.
Ceilings and floors:
- 9-10: he'd ship it untouched. Rare. It has to pass all three questions.
- 7: he'd build from it. The reference or the take is right, the wording isn't.
- 5-6: competent caption. Clever description of the situation. Nobody laughs.
- 3-4: "is SO back", "szn", "is BACK baby", "category 4 is a lifestyle", any noun-plus-vibe, any meta about betting or odds.
Mode weights: American-sports comparisons and unfair-at-a-place lines are underrated by default, score them up. Impressions (a politician's voice) are good but only if the words are funny without the voice. The "my group chat" / "me, to my roommate" self-narration device is a formula: cap at 5. "in other news" cap at 6 unless the news is genuinely the joke.
Length is not a fault. Explanation is. A flat tag after the punchline is not explanation.
A film or TV quote that is only the quote caps at 8; the reference has to do something to the bet. On a tie between a first-person confession and a sports or board-game comparison, take the comparison, and prefer "you" over "i". A concrete picture beats a named emotion ("seething" tells the joke; a lift line that starts in another state's car park shows it). The bet's own number turned into something else (a phone number, an SAT score, a payout per American) is a top-tier move even when it is juvenile.

PICKING FOR THE BOARD
After scoring, pick exactly one candidate per market under these board rules:
- no two picks may share a punchword (the distinctive word carrying the joke)
- the picks must span at least 4 modes; no more than 3 picks in any one mode
- when two candidates tie, take the riskier one, not the safer one
